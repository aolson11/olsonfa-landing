Two Decisions Must Stay Separate

Your immigration strategy is a legal matter. Your franchise choice is a business and ownership decision. Treating either one as automatic creates avoidable risk.

Investment Fit

Does the total investment, working-capital need, and downside exposure fit your actual resources?

Operating Role

Does the owner's required day-to-day role fit your experience, family needs, and desired level of involvement?

Business Fundamentals

Do the territory, unit economics, support model, and contractual obligations withstand independent review?

A Clear Three-Part Process

Keep legal advice, business evaluation, and final commitment in the correct order.

1

Obtain Independent Immigration Advice

Ask a qualified immigration attorney to assess your circumstances, eligibility, timing, and legal requirements before you rely on any business option.

2

Define the Business Criteria

Clarify investment capacity, operating role, geography, timeline, family constraints, and the outcomes ownership must produce.

3

Evaluate Specific Franchise Models

Compare the business facts, validate assumptions, review the Franchise Disclosure Document with appropriate professional advisors, and coordinate factual information with your attorney.

The Goal Is a Defensible Decision

A sound process does not promise an immigration outcome. It helps you determine whether a particular business deserves further legal, financial, and operational diligence.

Decision Clarity

Know why a franchise does or does not fit your objectives.

Advisor Alignment

Keep business facts and legal advice clearly separated and coordinated.

Documented Assumptions

Identify what must be verified before capital is committed.

Do Not Commit Capital Based on a Visa Marketing Claim

No franchise advisor can guarantee E-2 eligibility, approval, renewal, or permanent residency. Verify legal requirements with independent immigration counsel and business claims through appropriate diligence.
