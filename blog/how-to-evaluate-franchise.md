# How to Evaluate a Franchise Opportunity (JD's Checklist)

**Buying a franchise is not like buying a house or a stock.** You're not just assessing an asset; you're entering a business relationship that will last 10-20 years. That requires a different kind of due diligence. One most first-time buyers never learn until it's too late.

## The Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) . Your Primary Tool

Every legitimate franchisor must provide an FDD. It's 100+ pages of required disclosures. Here's what to focus on:

### Item 1: The Franchisor
- How long have they been in business?
- Any litigation history? (Red flag if frequent)
- Bankruptcy history of the company or its executives
- **What I look for:** Stability and transparency. Companies with clean histories and straightforward leadership are lower risk.

### Item 3: Litigation
- Suits against the franchisor by franchisees or customers
- Arbitration requirements (limits your ability to sue)
- **Red flags:** Frequent franchisee lawsuits, mandatory arbitration clauses that favor the franchisor

### Item 4: Bankruptcy
- Bankruptcy history of the company or its executives
- **What I look for:** Any bankruptcy in the last 10 years requires serious scrutiny

### Item 5: Restrictions on Where You Can Operate
- Territory definitions (exclusive vs. non-exclusive)
- Demographic requirements
- Right of first refusal for new locations
- **Critical question:** Does the territory protect you from competition, or is it just a sales pitch?

### Item 7: Initial Fees and Other Costs
- Franchise fee (a modest amount-a modest amount typical)
- Build-out costs (varies wildly by industry)
- Initial inventory and supplies
- Working capital requirements
- **What I look for:** Realistic cost estimates, not optimistic projections

### Item 8: Training and Support
- Duration and location of initial training
- Ongoing support services
- Marketing assistance
- **Red flags:** Vague descriptions, minimal training hours, no ongoing support mentioned

### Item 19: Financial Performance Representations (The Gold Mine)
- Actual revenue and profit data from existing franchisees
- Sample size and selection methodology
- Time period covered
- **What I look for:** Transparent data, not just best-case scenarios. If they don't provide this, that's a red flag.

### Item 20: Franchisee Outlet Information
- Total number of franchises
- Number sold in the last 3 years
- Number closed/terminated in the last 3 years
- **Critical calculation:** Closure rate. More than 15% closure rate in 3 years is concerning.

## The Due Diligence Process . Step by Step

### Phase 1: Initial Screening (Weeks 1-2)
1. **Request the FDD** . Legitimate franchisors will provide it promptly
2. **Review Items 1-5** . Basic company health check
3. **Check Item 20** . Closure rates and growth trends
4. **Verify claims independently** . Don't take their word for anything

### Phase 2: Deep Dive (Weeks 3-6)
1. **Study Item 19 carefully** . Talk to franchisees listed in the data
2. **Visit multiple locations** . Not just the "show" location they want you to see
3. **Talk to current franchisees** . Ask about profitability, support quality, and challenges
4. **Get expert review** . Have a qualified professional review the FDD (I recommend this for every client)

### Phase 3: Financial Modeling (Weeks 7-8)
1. **Create your own projections** . Don't rely on franchisor numbers alone
2. **Stress test assumptions** . What if revenue is 20% lower than projected?
3. **Calculate break-even timeline** . How long until you're profitable?
4. **Plan for worst case** . Can you survive 18 months of losses?

### Phase 4: Final Evaluation (Weeks 9-10)
1. **Compare multiple opportunities** . Never evaluate one franchise in isolation
2. **Assess your fit** . Does this match your skills, budget, and goals?
3. **Make a decision** . Based on data, not emotions or pressure

## The Questions Most Buyers Don't Ask (But Should)

### To the Franchisor
- "What percentage of your franchisees are profitable?"
- "Can you introduce me to 5 current franchisees I can talk to independently?"
- "What's your average franchisee's revenue in year 3?"
- "How do you handle territory conflicts?"

### To Current Franchisees (When You Meet Them)
- "What surprised you most about owning this franchise?"
- "What would you do differently if you could start over?"
- "How responsive is the franchisor when problems arise?"
- "Are you profitable? If not, why not?"

### To Yourself
- "Am I evaluating this objectively or because I want it to work?"
- "What's my exit strategy if this doesn't work out?"
- "Can I afford to lose everything I invest here?"
- "Does this align with my long-term goals or just solve a short-term problem?"

## Red Flags That Should Make You Walk Away

1. **Pressure to sign quickly** . "This opportunity won't last" is never true
2. **Reluctance to provide FDD** . Legitimate franchisors expect you to review it
3. **Unrealistic profit claims** . If it sounds too good, it is
4. **High closure rates** . More than 15% in 3 years is concerning
5. **Litigation history with franchisees** . Pattern of disputes is a warning sign
6. **Vague territory definitions** . "Non-exclusive" territories offer little protection
7. **No Item 19 data** . If they won't share financial performance, why should you trust them?

## The Green Flags That Signal a Good Fit

1. **Transparent FDD** . Clear, honest disclosures without hiding anything
2. **Strong franchisee network** . Willingness to connect you with existing owners
3. **Realistic projections** . Based on actual data, not best-case scenarios
4. **Solid training program** . Comprehensive and ongoing support
5. **Fair territory protection** . Clear definitions that actually protect your investment
6. **Reasonable fees** . Not the cheapest option, but fair for what you're getting
7. **Long track record** . At least 5-10 years in business with growing franchisee base
