"Franchise Due Diligence Checklist: 25 Questions Every Buyer Must Ask"

By Austin Olson, JDJune 26, 2026

Franchise Due Diligence Checklist: 25 Questions Every Buyer Must Ask

Most first-time buyers sign franchise agreements without asking the questions that actually matter. They get excited about the brand, skip the hard conversations, and discover too late what should have been obvious from day one.

I've reviewed hundreds of franchise opportunities and helped dozens of professionals evaluate them. The difference between informed buyers and costly mistakes always comes down to these 25 questions.

franchise buyers skip critical due diligence steps because they're excited about the opportunity or pressured to move quickly. Don't be that person. Here's exactly what you need to investigate before signing any agreement.

Questions for the Franchisor (During Sales Meetings)

1. "How many franchises do you currently have, and how many have closed in the last 3 years?"

Why it matters: Closure rate reveals franchise health. More than 15% closure in 3 years is concerning.

2. "Can you provide a list of current franchisees I can contact independently?"

Red flag: If they refuse or only provide their own references, walk away. You need to hear from owners on your own terms.

3. "What's the average revenue and profit for franchisees in year 1, year 3, and year 5?"

Why it matters: Realistic projections help you plan financially. Vague answers mean they're hiding something.

4. "How do you define territory? Is it exclusive or non-exclusive?"

Critical question: Non-exclusive territories offer little protection from competition. Get specifics on boundaries and population thresholds.

5. "What happens if I want to sell my franchise? What are the transfer fees and requirements?"

Why it matters: You need an exit strategy. Restrictive transfer terms can trap you in a business you no longer want.

6. "How often do you update the operations manual, and what's the process for implementing changes?"

Why it matters: Franchises that don't evolve become obsolete. Regular updates show commitment to franchisee success.

7. "What marketing support do you provide, and how is the national advertising fund used?"

Red flag: If they can't explain exactly where your advertising dollars go, be cautious.

8. "Can you walk me through your training program in detail?"

Why it matters: Comprehensive training is essential for first-time buyers. Vague descriptions mean inadequate support.

Questions for Existing Franchisees (When You Meet Them)

9. "How long have you owned this franchise, and are you profitable?"

Listen for: Honest answers about challenges, not just success stories. Ask specifically about their worst month.

10. "What surprised you most about owning this franchise that wasn't covered in the FDD?"

Why it matters: This reveals hidden costs or challenges that franchisors don't disclose.

11. "How responsive is the franchisor when problems arise? Give me a specific example."

Listen for: Specific examples, not general praise. Look for evidence of genuine support during difficulties.

12. "What would you do differently if you could start over?"

Why it matters: This reveals the franchise's biggest weaknesses from an insider's perspective.

13. "How accurate were the financial projections in the FDD? What was your actual experience?"

Critical question: Many franchisors' projections are optimistic. Real data from existing owners is invaluable.

Questions for Yourself (Before You Invest)

14. "Can I afford to lose everything I invest here and still be financially stable?"

The hard truth: If the answer is no, you're taking on too much risk. Conservative planning saves businesses.

15. "Am I evaluating this objectively or because I want it to work?"

Psychological check: We all want our investments to succeed. But wishful thinking leads to costly mistakes. Be honest about your biases.

16. "What's my exit strategy if this doesn't work out as planned?"

Every investment needs an exit. Without one, you're gambling, not investing.

17. "Does this franchise align with my skills, interests, and long-term goals?"

Fit matters: Even a great franchise won't succeed if it doesn't match your strengths and passions.

18. "Am I prepared for the time commitment required, especially in the first 2-3 years?"

Reality check: Most franchises require 50-60 hours per week initially. If you can't commit that time, find a different model.

Financial Due Diligence Questions

19. "What are all the costs associated with this franchise, including hidden fees?"

Get everything in writing: Franchise fee, build-out, equipment, inventory, working capital, royalties, marketing fees, technology fees, insurance, legal/accounting.

20. "What financing options do you recommend, and have you worked with these lenders before?"

Why it matters: Some franchisors have relationships with SBA lenders who understand franchise lending. This can streamline your financing process.

21. "What's the break-even timeline for typical franchisees in my market?"

Critical question: Revenue projections mean nothing without understanding when you'll actually become profitable.

Legal and Contract Questions

22. "Should I have someone review the FDD and franchise agreement before I sign?"

Non-negotiable: Never sign a franchise agreement without independent franchise counsel. The cost ($a modest investment-$a modest investment) is tiny compared to potential mistakes.

23. "What dispute resolution mechanisms are in the contract? Arbitration or litigation?"

Why it matters: Mandatory arbitration limits your ability to sue. Understand your rights before committing.

24. "What happens if I want to terminate the agreement early? What are the penalties?"

Exit strategy: Understanding termination terms helps you evaluate long-term commitment risks.

The Final Question (Most Important)

25. "If this were my money and my future, would I still invest in this franchise?"

The gut check: Strip away all the sales pressure and excitement. Look at the facts objectively. Does this opportunity genuinely make sense for your situation?

Red Flags That Should Make You Walk Away

  1. Pressure to sign quickly . "This opportunity won't last" is never true
  2. Reluctance to provide FDD or franchisee contacts . Legitimate franchisors expect due diligence
  3. Unrealistic profit claims . If it sounds too good, it is
  4. High closure rates . More than 15% in 3 years is concerning
  5. Litigation history with franchisees . Pattern of disputes is a warning sign
  6. Vague territory definitions . "Non-exclusive" territories offer little protection
  7. No Item 19 financial data . If they won't share performance data, why should you trust them?

Green Flags That Signal a Good Fit

  1. Transparent FDD . Clear, honest disclosures without hiding anything
  2. Strong franchisee network . Willingness to connect you with existing owners
  3. Realistic projections . Based on actual data, not best-case scenarios
  4. Solid training program . Comprehensive and ongoing support
  5. Fair territory protection . Clear definitions that actually protect your investment
  6. Reasonable fees . Not the cheapest option, but fair for what you're getting
  7. Long track record . At least 5-10 years in business with growing franchisee base

The Due Diligence Timeline

Phase Duration What You'll Do
Initial screening 1-2 weeks Request FDD, review basic company health
Deep dive 3-6 weeks Study Item 19, visit locations, talk to franchisees
Financial modeling 2-3 weeks Create projections, stress-test assumptions
Expert review 1-2 weeks Have a qualified professional review FDD and agreement
Final evaluation 1 week Compare multiple opportunities, make decision

Total: 8-14 weeks of thorough due diligence. Rushing this process is the #1 mistake first-time buyers make.
